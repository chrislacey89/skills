# An authored mutation cannot test the shape its author did not imagine

## Problem

A detector goes in, and a mutation battery goes in beside it to prove the detector fires. Every mutation goes red. The detector is still blind to the failure shape that actually occurs in the repository — because the mutations and the detector were generated by one mind, from one model of how the thing breaks, and they can only agree.

## Context

PR #311 (issue #310) fixed a `/closeout` line that emitted `origin/prod` where its own comment claimed `prod`, and added a census to `scripts/test-documented-git-commands.sh` so the line would be *run* rather than grepped. The census partitions every `origin/HEAD` occurrence into `fenced` (an agent copies and runs it) and `prose` (a citation), runs the fenced ones, and requires the prose ones to be genuine citations.

The prose half needed a discriminator, and it took three drafts:

1. *"Does the line still say something once its inline-code spans are removed?"* A bare recipe has no spans, so nothing was removed and every bare recipe passed as prose.
2. *"Is there a backtick before the signal and one after it?"* — the fix for (1).
3. *"Strip balanced spans and require the signal to vanish."* — the fix for (2).

Draft (1) was caught by a mutation the author wrote: **unfence the line**. Draft (2) was not, and could not have been, because the same author wrote the mutation for it — and the mutation planted this:

```
git symbolic-ref refs/remotes/origin/HEAD --short   # e.g. origin/main → main
```

A bare recipe with nothing around it. Draft (2) correctly flags that. What draft (2) does *not* flag is what real prose in this repository looks like:

> Use the `base` value: run git symbolic-ref refs/remotes/origin/HEAD --short to get it, then hand it to `git switch`.

Every paragraph here is dense with inline code, so a copyable recipe dropped mid-sentence is almost always straddled by two unrelated spans — and draft (2) reads a backtick before and a backtick after and calls it a citation. A review sub-agent with no memory of the drafts planted exactly that line in its first pass.

The author's battery was six mutations, all red. The count was not the problem.

## Symptoms

- A mutation battery is described by its **count** — "six mutations, all red" — rather than by where each fixture came from.
- Every fixture in the battery is one the author wrote, and each is **simpler and cleaner** than anything the corpus actually contains.
- The detector and the fixtures share a vocabulary: both are phrased in terms of the same handful of tokens the author was thinking in.
- A reviewer with no memory of the drafts breaks the detector on the first realistic input.
- The battery's red sweep is cited as evidence of **generality**, when it is only evidence of **agreement**.

## Root Cause

**A mutation is a hypothesis about how the thing breaks, and the author has exactly one.** Writing the detector requires forming a model of the failure. Writing the mutation requires the same model — and there is no second model available in that session, so the fixture is drawn from the model rather than from the world. Every fixture therefore lands inside the detector's coverage by construction, and the sweep goes red for the same reason a tautology is true.

This is `self-review-blind-to-composition`'s root — *the blindness and the sight are the same mechanism seen from two sides* — relocated from review to mutation testing, where it is worse, because mutation testing is the technique people reach for **precisely when they distrust a green run**. It converts "I checked" into "a mechanism checked" while the mechanism is still the author.

The corpus is the second model, and it is free. It is sitting in the repository the check is about to run against.

## Learning Level

- **Level:** Structure
- **Feedback loop or delay:** a balancing loop whose sensor is wired to its own actuator. The battery is supposed to measure the detector's coverage; instead both are outputs of one model, so the measurement tracks the model rather than the population. Confidence rises with every red mutation while true coverage stays wherever the author's imagination left it, and there is no crossover signal — a battery that fully agrees with a narrow detector looks identical to one that fully agrees with a general one.

## Rule Scope

- **Applies when** all three hold:
  - the check is a **detector or predicate over a corpus** — a partition, a classifier, a lint, a grep-shaped guard — rather than an assertion about one known value;
  - the **same session** authors both the check and the mutations that validate it; and
  - the corpus **already exists** and contains real instances of the input class.
- **Inverts or does not apply when:**
  - **The corpus is empty.** For a check written before any instance exists — a guard against a shape the repo has never had — there is nothing to draw from, and a composed fixture is the only option. Declare it synthetic in the test's comment; that is the honest form, not a defect.
  - **The check asserts one known value.** `assert_eq 4 "$(add 2 2)"` has no input class to be blind to, and `/tdd`'s red-bar rule already covers it.
  - **The mutation targets the fixture's oracle rather than the subject.** That is `mutate-the-oracle-not-only-the-subject`'s territory, and drawing from the corpus does not help there.
  - **A second, intent-blind reader writes the battery.** The active ingredient is the single model, not the authoring per se — the same person writing the battery a week later, having forgotten the drafts, is a weaker but real second model.
- **Sibling docs:**
  - `mechanism-generality-lags-the-pattern-2026-08-23.md` — **this incident is also an instance of that entry**, and the more important relationship. See Defect Clustering below.
  - `../architecture-decisions/self-review-blind-to-composition-2026-08-13.md` — the same root at the review layer. That entry's fix is a second reader; this one's is a second *source of inputs*, which is cheaper and available with no reviewer at all.
  - `dead-guards-report-coverage-they-do-not-have-2026-08-27.md` — a guard that cannot fire. This is the next case out: a guard that *can* fire, on inputs that do not occur.
  - `mutate-the-oracle-not-only-the-subject-2026-08-19.md` — mutate the right *artifact*. This entry is about planting the right *shape* in it.
  - `validate-the-instrument-not-only-the-subject-2026-08-23.md` — the family root.

## Solution

**Take a real line out of the tree, perturb it minimally, and use that.**

**Before** — the fixture composed from the author's model of a bare recipe:

```bash
# mutation: unfence /closeout's line
git symbolic-ref refs/remotes/origin/HEAD --short   # e.g. origin/main → main
```

Clean, minimal, and unlike any prose in the repository. It goes red against a detector that is blind to the real shape.

**After** — the fixture taken from the corpus, then perturbed. The realistic line is straddled by two spans, which is what actual paragraphs here look like:

```bash
# shellcheck disable=SC2016  # fixtures are literal markdown, not expansions
bare_recipe='Use the `base` value: run git symbolic-ref refs/remotes/origin/HEAD --short to get it, then hand it to `git switch`.'
cited_recipe='The repo declares its base (`git symbolic-ref refs/remotes/origin/HEAD`). Do not assume `main`.'
if signal_is_inline_code "$bare_recipe"; then
    fatal "the inline-code detector calls a bare straddled recipe 'prose' — the prose branch below now passes every copyable recipe."
fi
```

And the detector that survives it — strip balanced spans, require the signal to **vanish**:

```bash
signal_is_inline_code() {
    # shellcheck disable=SC2016  # the backticks are markdown delimiters in a sed script
    ! printf '%s' "$1" | sed 's/`[^`]*`//g' | grep -qF "$name_signal"
}
```

A second, smaller finding from the same pass, kept because its detector is the same idea applied to the *suite* rather than to the code: **a suite that dies mid-run prints no failure.** The comment-claim check shipped a no-match `grep` inside a command substitution under `set -o pipefail`, so the run ended at the first site whose line carried no claim — no assertion, no message, and `| tail -1 | grep passed` returning empty. That empty output was read as "fine" twice. The corpus-drawn fixture for a suite is its **exit status**, not its stdout:

```bash
bash scripts/test-documented-git-commands.sh; echo "SUITE_EXIT=$?"
```

The file's own extractor documents this exact pipefail defect twenty lines above where it recurred. Reading the warning is not running the check.

## Prevention

**Code-level.** Both detectors in the new census now carry a self-test — a fixture they MUST flag and a corrected fixture they MUST NOT — in the shape `scripts/test-guards-can-fire.sh` uses, and the flag-fixture is the corpus-drawn straddled line above. Blinding either detector, or making one reject its own corrected form, now aborts the run. This is `mechanism-generality-lags-the-pattern` Prevention #2 applied: *a detector whose healthy state is zero hits needs a self-test, not a floor.*

Also from the same pass, and the same idea one level up: a floor over a whole population is not a floor over any site in it. The census's `-ge 6` let a seventh claim planted anywhere mask the deletion of the one site the section exists for. It is now per-site over a derived list.

**Process-level.** `/execute` Step 4's existing mutation rung now carries the rule: *draw the edit from the corpus, not from your model of the failure* — take a real line from the tree the check will run against and perturb it minimally, and where the corpus holds no instance, declare the fixture synthetic in the test's comment. It is placed at that rung rather than in a new one because the rung already asks the right question ("does the census cover the right set?") and was answered with a fixture that could not test it.

## Defect Clustering

**This incident is a recurrence of `mechanism-generality-lags-the-pattern-2026-08-23.md`** — same `category`, and its `problem_type` (*"a mechanism built to catch a pattern is written against the syntax of the instance that produced it, so the pattern recurs in a different shape and the mechanism stays silent"*) describes prose-discriminator draft (2) exactly. That entry was prose plus one mechanism, and the pattern recurred anyway, in this repo, five days later. So per `/compound` Phase 4 this entry ships a mechanism rather than more prose: the two detector self-tests committed in `scripts/test-documented-git-commands.sh`, plus the `/execute` Step 4 rule above.

**It is filed separately rather than as an update to that entry** because the transferable rule is different and neither implies the other. That entry says *the mechanism you write will be narrower than the pattern*. This one says *the mutation you write to check that mechanism is drawn from the same model, so it cannot reveal the narrowness*. You can commit either without the other: a corpus-drawn battery on a general detector still passes, and a composed battery on a narrow detector still goes red. Cross-referenced in both directions.

## Planning / Calibration Notes

- **What widened the work:** two review-driven fix rounds after the first "verified" pass. The first pass shipped six red mutations and a green suite and was wrong in three places; two of the three were the check's own defect class one layer in.
- **What tightened the work:** an intent-blind review sub-agent, which found all three in a single pass by planting one realistic line and reproducing the floor's slack. The cost asymmetry is the point — it took one delegation and returned the defects the author's battery was structurally unable to reach.
- **Future planning adjustment:** when a slice's deliverable *is* a detector, budget for the corpus-drawn battery and for one intent-blind pass over it. The author's own mutation sweep should not be treated as the verification step for that class of slice — it is a necessary first pass, not evidence of coverage.

## Actuals Worth Reusing

- **Comparable future work:** any slice whose deliverable is a `scripts/test-*.sh` detector, partition, or census over repository text.
- **Reusable baseline:** the check itself was ~30 lines; three drafts of one predicate and three review-found defects sat on top of it. For this class, assume the detector is the small part and its validation is the work.
